## Separate the position from the money supporting it

Position value describes the market exposure represented by the contract quantity. Margin is the amount supporting that position under the account's rules. Leverage describes a relationship between exposure and supporting capital; it does not replace the position value in every cost calculation.

WEEX's [trading-fee calculation guide](https://www.weex.com/help/articles/4415852872601) states that futures trading fees are based on position value rather than leverage. It also distinguishes maker and taker rates and notes that fees arise on executed opening, closing or reducing activity, rather than on an order that remains unfilled.

The practical implication is straightforward: record the executed quantity, price, contract specification and applicable rate. Do not begin with the margin figure merely because it is the most prominent number in the order screen. The account's current contract and fee information should supply the relevant inputs.

## Hold exposure constant in a comparison

Take an entirely hypothetical position with a value of 10,000 USDT and an assumed transaction-fee rate of 0.05%. The fee for the execution would be 5 USDT. Now imagine two different margin amounts supporting that same exposure: 2,000 USDT in one case and 1,000 USDT in another.

The 5-USDT fee is 0.25% of the first margin amount and 0.50% of the second. Its size relative to the margin changed, but its monetary amount did not. Describing the second setup as cheaper merely because it uses less margin would confuse capital allocation with transaction cost.

If a confirmed referral arrangement reduced the entire eligible 5-USDT fee by 20%, the reduction would be 1 USDT and the remaining fee would be 4 USDT. That remaining fee would equal 0.20% of the first margin amount or 0.40% of the second. The example does not quote WEEX's current rates or establish personal eligibility through gfek.

## A different comparison changes the exposure

Suppose instead that someone keeps the same margin amount but increases the position value. That is no longer a comparison of identical exposure under different margin settings. The fee can increase because the executed value has increased, even if the percentage fee rate stays the same.

The same larger exposure also changes how market movements affect the position. A referral reduction in a covered fee does not neutralize that additional risk. It is particularly misleading to compare only the amount “saved” while omitting the larger gross fee or the larger potential loss.

When reviewing two setups, write down what remains constant and what changes. Compare like with like. If the position size, product, execution type or holding period differs, identify the difference before attributing the result to a referral code.

## Use the eligible fee as the percentage base

The supplied gfek offer concerns eligible trading fees. A 20% fee reduction does not mean that 20% of deposited capital is paid back. It also does not imply a 20-percentage-point reduction in a trading-fee rate.

For example, under a hypothetical 0.05% starting fee, a confirmed 20% reduction would leave 80% of that fee, or an effective 0.04% if applied directly to the full charge. The reduction is 0.01 percentage points. Keeping percentages and percentage points distinct avoids claims that are impossible under the actual arithmetic.

If the account receives a later eligible credit instead of an immediately lower charge, preserve the original charge and record the credit separately. That makes it possible to reconcile the account without counting the same benefit twice or assuming a credit has arrived before it appears.

## Funding requires its own calculation

WEEX's [funding-fee documentation](https://www.weex.com/help/articles/4410862743449) describes funding as payments between position holders, based on the funding rate and position value. It explains that settlement schedules can vary by pair and that the relevant holding status at settlement matters.

Do not replace position value with posted margin in a funding estimate either. More importantly, do not assume the referral's transaction-fee offer changes funding payments. That would require explicit terms, not an inference from the word “fees.”

For an existing position, use the contract's current schedule and the payments actually recorded. For a possible future holding period, label any funding calculation as a scenario because rates, values and the position's holding duration can change. A favorable past payment does not establish a future income stream.
