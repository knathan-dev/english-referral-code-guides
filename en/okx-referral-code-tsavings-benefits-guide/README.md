# OKX referral code TSAVINGS: up to 20% off eligible trading fees without counting the saving twice

OKX referral code TSAVINGS is supplied with **up to 20% off eligible trading fees**, subject to the actual rate, covered products and regional arrangement shown for your account. Follow the [OKX invitation](https://okx.com/join/TSAVINGS) and establish those terms first. The central task here is reading a fee statement correctly: a displayed net fee, a separate credit and an advertised percentage are not automatically three separate benefits.

## Find out what the displayed number represents

Before calculating anything, ask whether the account shows a starting charge or a charge after adjustments. A label such as “fee” can be insufficient by itself. The same-looking number can play a different role in different reports or account arrangements.

Write down the explanation supplied by the operator. If the rate already incorporates the applicable referral treatment, applying the full percentage again would understate the cost. If the arrangement instead provides a separate eligible credit, the original charge and that credit need their own entries.

## Keep regional pricing in the calculation

OKX publishes guidance for EEA trading fees, illustrating why a generic global assumption is not a reliable substitute for the account's applicable schedule. Read the guidance relevant to your account and product rather than borrowing a rate from someone else's screenshot.

A referral offer does not establish that every regional account has the same starting rate or treatment. The useful evidence is the schedule and arrangement that actually apply to you. If a regional page and an account display appear inconsistent, resolve that difference before building a projected saving around either number.

## Work through the double-counting trap

Imagine a hypothetical starting charge of 7.50 units and a confirmed 20% reduction. The saving is 1.50 and the remaining charge is six. If a statement already displays six as the final fee, subtracting a further 1.20 would incorrectly report a cost of 4.80.

Alternatively, a record might display 7.50 charged and 1.50 credited separately. The economic cost would still be six once the credit is actually received. These two presentation methods can describe the same outcome. The presence of two numbers does not establish two independent promotions.

## Check the scope before combining records

Accounts, products and periods may have different applicable conditions. Keep them separate until you have a reason to combine them. This matters when one report includes several kinds of activity but the referral terms cover only some of the charges.

The official account guidance also distinguishes account modes and subaccount arrangements. Do not infer referral coverage merely because balances appear under a common login. Ask which specific account context receives the benefit and whether the report you are using contains that context's eligible charges.

## Resolve uncertainty with a concrete question

A precise support question might be: “Does the fee shown for this execution already include the referral arrangement, or should a separate credit appear?” Include the relevant execution and period without disclosing credentials or unnecessary personal information.

Avoid asking only whether TSAVINGS is a valid string. Valid attribution and correct accounting are related but different issues. You need both the invitation association and an explanation of how its applicable benefit reaches the account before you can reconcile the result confidently.

## Save a reusable calculation note

Once clarified, write a brief formula in ordinary language: starting eligible charge minus confirmed separate credit, or final displayed charge with no further subtraction. Attach the applicable date and product so the note remains interpretable later.

Revisit that note when the terms or report layout change. A formula that was correct last month can become misleading if its inputs change meaning. The goal is not to preserve a particular percentage in a spreadsheet; it is to preserve a defensible account of the amount actually paid.
