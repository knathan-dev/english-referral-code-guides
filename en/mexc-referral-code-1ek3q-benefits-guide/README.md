# MEXC referral code 1Ek3q: up to 20% off eligible trading fees when the starting charge matters

MEXC referral code 1Ek3q is supplied with **up to 20% off eligible trading fees**, where the current account and selected activity qualify. Check the live arrangement through the [MEXC invitation](https://www.mexc.com/invite/register?inviteCode=1Ek3q). The starting charge is essential: a percentage reduction on a fee that is already zero produces no additional fee saving. This guide shows how to compare different fee situations without turning the referral headline into an unsupported cash estimate.

## Identify the charge before applying a percentage

Write down the fee that would actually apply to the intended transaction under current account conditions. Do not select a higher published rate merely because it makes a proposed discount look more impressive. The relevant baseline is the charge applicable to the same activity without the specific benefit being evaluated.

If the baseline is unclear, pause the savings calculation. You can still examine order instructions and product suitability, but an unknown starting fee cannot support a precise claim that a referral will save a particular amount.

## Keep zero-fee and positive-fee activity apart

A hypothetical period might contain one group of eligible transactions with zero fees and another group with twelve units of eligible fees. At a confirmed 20% benefit on the positive-fee group, the saving would be 2.40 units. The zero-fee group adds nothing to that amount.

A large transaction value does not change this arithmetic. Multiplying zero by twenty percent remains zero. This is why a report that only lists total trading volume is insufficient for estimating a referral saving: it omits the actual charge and eligibility of each relevant portion.

## Avoid comparing different baskets as if they were identical

One month may contain mostly zero-fee activity, while another includes products or conditions with positive charges. The second month could generate a larger referral saving and still cost more overall. A rising discount total therefore does not necessarily represent an improvement.

Compare the remaining eligible cost and the activity behind it. If your purpose or product mix changed, say so in the comparison. The cleanest explanation may be that you undertook a different set of transactions, not that the invitation became more generous.

## Do not add activity merely to make a benefit visible

It can feel disappointing when a promotional percentage produces no extra saving on an already free charge. That is not a problem that needs fixing with additional transactions. A fee benefit has value only in relation to activity that is otherwise appropriate.

Market exposure and execution costs do not disappear because an explicit commission is low or zero. MEXC's official market-order guidance helps explain execution mechanics. Review those mechanics for the intended order, and keep the decision to trade separate from the desire to see a referral credit in a statement.

## Recheck the baseline when conditions change

An estimate should state its date and assumptions. If current terms, product selection or account conditions change, rebuild the estimate from the new starting charge. Do not keep applying a previous zero-fee assumption or a previous positive rate merely because it is already entered in a spreadsheet.

Likewise, do not assume that a referral and every other promotion combine. Ask which arrangement applies and whether the displayed fee already incorporates it. The answer determines whether there is a remaining fee on which the supplied percentage could operate.

## Make the final comparison easy to audit

A short explanation can include four facts: the intended activity, the applicable starting charge, the confirmed benefit and the remaining cost. That is enough to make a modest calculation understandable without presenting speculative future savings.

For a hypothetical five-unit eligible fee, a confirmed 20% benefit reduces the cost by one unit. If the account instead qualifies for 6%, the reduction is 0.30. Keep the lower confirmed figure rather than the higher advertised maximum when describing the result actually available to you.
