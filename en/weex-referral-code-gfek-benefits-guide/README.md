## Start with a reason for each transaction

A transaction should have a purpose that exists independently of the referral. Write down the decision it implements and the condition that would make it unnecessary. This creates a useful pause before repeated adjustments begin to look like progress simply because they generate activity.

The invitation changes neither the need for a coherent plan nor the uncertainty of market outcomes. Even if an eligible fee is reduced, an unnecessary transaction still adds an execution event and may add cost. The correct comparison is with the appropriate alternative, which may be doing nothing.

## Define turnover in a practical way

For this review, use turnover to mean the total value of the relevant completed transactions over a chosen period. State exactly what you include. Do not substitute the amount deposited or the account's ending balance for the activity measure.

## Set a review threshold rather than a trading target

A personal fee threshold can trigger a pause and review. For example, you might decide to reassess the plan when cumulative eligible charges reach a chosen amount. The threshold is not an instruction to keep trading until that amount has been spent.

Choose a level you can understand in the context of your overall budget. If activity reaches it unexpectedly, inspect the sequence of decisions that produced the cost. Look for repeated changes of direction, duplicated intentions or assumptions that no longer match the current plan.

## Distinguish fee savings from trading results

A reduced commission is a cost adjustment. It does not establish that the underlying activity was profitable or appropriate. A hypothetical position can lose much more from price movement than the commission saved through a referral.

Keep the fee analysis separate from the result of the trading decision. This makes both clearer: you can confirm that a discount was applied correctly while also recognizing that the activity itself did not meet your objectives. Neither conclusion needs to be distorted to support the other.

## Review the pattern, not just the final total

At the end of a period, examine where the activity accumulated. A small number of planned transactions and a long series of reactive adjustments can produce similar totals while reflecting very different decision processes. Identify which pattern actually occurred.

Use that review to improve the next period's process rather than to invent a guaranteed outcome. The referral benefit can remain in the cost estimate where confirmed, but it should not become a justification for increasing turnover or ignoring the reasons behind the transactions.
