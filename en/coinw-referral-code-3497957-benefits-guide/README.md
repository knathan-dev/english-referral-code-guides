## Distinguish the spending limit from the transferred amount

Your total spending limit is a personal boundary. The amount arriving at an account is an operational fact. They can differ because a funding route may involve costs or because you deliberately retain some funds elsewhere. Do not let a promotional percentage blur that distinction.

Set the limit before deciding on the transfer. If the account's requirements remain unclear, you can gather information without increasing the amount committed. A referral saving should improve an eligible cost within the plan, not silently enlarge the plan itself.

## Allocate a reserve before assigning every unit

A reserve is simply an amount you choose not to commit immediately. Its appropriate size depends on your circumstances and the costs you have actually identified; there is no universal percentage established by this guide.

In a hypothetical hundred-unit budget, someone might allocate eighty-five to an intended activity and retain fifteen for flexibility. Those numbers illustrate separation, not a recommendation. The reserve should not be justified by an assumption that a referral benefit will later refill it. An expected saving is not a confirmed incoming balance.
