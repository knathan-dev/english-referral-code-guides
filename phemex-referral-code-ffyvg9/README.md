## Define what you are comparing

A useful comparison specifies the asset you want to receive, the amount, the starting asset or currency, and the proposed method. Without those details, two “deposit fee” claims may describe entirely different transactions.

Sending a crypto asset that you already own is different from purchasing that asset through a payment provider. Moving funds internally between two accounts on the same platform is different from an on-chain withdrawal. A bank's own charge can also sit outside the receiving platform's advertised fee.

## Compare the quoted payment with the credited result

For a purchase, review both the total amount payable and the amount of the asset offered in return. Check whether charges appear separately or are reflected in the quoted conversion. A prominently displayed percentage can be less useful than the complete quote.

For a transfer of an asset you already hold, compare the amount deducted at the sending side with the expected amount received, while accounting for any charge paid in another asset. Keep the units consistent. A fee paid in a network's native asset should not be silently added to a token amount as though they were the same currency.

This method does not require choosing the smallest visible fee at all costs. Compatibility, availability and correct recipient information come first. A cheaper route that cannot deliver the intended asset is not a valid alternative.

## Use an example with explicit assumptions

Imagine two hypothetical methods that both deliver the same asset under otherwise comparable conditions. Method A requires 102 units of total payment to credit 100 units of value. Method B requires 103 units to credit the same amount. Under those assumptions, A has the lower immediate funding cost by one unit.

Now suppose the eventual eligible trading fee is 0.80 units and a confirmed referral arrangement reduces that fee by 20%. The reduction is 0.16 units, regardless of whether funding used A or B, provided the offer's conditions are otherwise identical. It does not retroactively eliminate the difference between the funding methods.

These numbers are illustrations, not quotes from Phemex or its providers. Real comparisons may involve different currencies, changing prices and different completion times. State those differences rather than pretending a precise comparison exists when the inputs are not comparable.

## Be careful with fixed transfer charges

A fixed charge represents a larger percentage of a small transfer than of a larger transfer. For example, a hypothetical one-unit charge is 5% of a 20-unit transfer but 0.5% of a 200-unit transfer. That arithmetic does not, on its own, justify transferring ten times as much.

Moving a larger amount changes how much money is held at the destination and can change your exposure to the asset or platform. Evaluate the amount you actually intended to fund. Repeatedly moving tiny amounts may be costly, but consolidating them should be a considered operational decision rather than a response to a promotional percentage.

Do not count an unneeded future trade as a benefit simply because it would generate an eligible fee reduction. The purpose of a fee offer is to reduce a covered cost within an otherwise sensible plan, not to create a reason to spend more.
