## Look beyond the first price level

An order book can contain several prices with different available quantities. A large purchase may consume the lowest available offers and continue into higher offers. The average paid price can then exceed the initial best ask. That difference is one way execution costs can arise without appearing as a separate commission line.

Consider a simplified hypothetical book. There are 40 units offered at 10.00 USDT and another 60 units at 10.05 USDT. Buying all 100 units would cost 1,003 USDT before commission, for an average price of 10.03 USDT. Assuming 100 units could all be bought at 10.00 would understate the purchase cost by 3 USDT.

Real order books change while orders are processed, so this is an arithmetic illustration rather than a forecast of any LBank execution. The visible depth is useful information, but it can change or disappear before your order reaches it.

## Choose execution rules that match your intention

A limit price can control the worst price you are willing to accept for an individual execution, but it may leave some or all of an order unfilled. A market instruction prioritizes access to available liquidity and can expose you to a changing average price. Neither method guarantees a favorable market outcome.

Decide whether immediate completion is necessary and how much price movement is acceptable before pressing submit. Reducing or splitting a large instruction can change execution, but it is not automatically beneficial: additional time, changing liquidity, and the total filled amount still matter.

Keep the investment decision separate from a desire to demonstrate savings. Trading more because the commission is discounted can increase total fees and market exposure. The useful comparison is the cost of the activity you actually intended to undertake.
