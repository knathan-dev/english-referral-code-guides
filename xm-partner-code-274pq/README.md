## Give the demo a specific job

XM's [account-types page](https://www.xm.com/account-types) offers demo practice with virtual funds and presents different live-account plans. The practical value of a demo is learning how controls and records work. It is less useful if the only conclusion is that a simulated balance increased.

Before practicing, choose a short list of questions. Can you identify the account currency? Can you distinguish balance, equity and margin information? Can you find the contract specification for the product being viewed? Can you tell whether a number represents lots, units or a monetary amount? These are concrete skills you can assess without committing funds.

Write the answer in your own words. If a term is unclear, return to the platform's help material rather than assuming its meaning from the size of the number. A large virtual balance can make order entry look forgiving even when you have misunderstood the exposure. Practice should reveal that misunderstanding before money is involved.

## Carry questions, not simulated profits, into registration

When considering a live account, reuse the checklist rather than projecting the demo's return. Confirm the account type, base currency and available instruments in the actual regional registration. Check the applicable spreads, commissions and overnight charges from the current specifications. A practice account's display is not a binding quote for every live account offered under the same brand.

Suppose you practiced with a round virtual balance of 10,000 units but intend to allocate a much smaller real amount. The relevant question is not how to reproduce the simulated profit. It is whether you understand the position size, possible loss and available margin in the account you would actually open. This is an illustrative learning comparison, not a suggested deposit size.

You may also find that a live account offering one set of costs has different promotional eligibility from another. Review those characteristics together. A larger bonus headline does not establish a lower overall trading cost or a better fit for your intended use.

Before accepting trading credit, identify which account receives it, what qualifying action is required, and what happens if funds are withdrawn or the promotion ends. Read the rules attached to the offer shown to you. Do not import a cap or deadline from an old campaign merely because its title also contains “100%.”

For a simple illustration, a hypothetical qualifying deposit of 200 units with a confirmed 100% credit would add 200 units of promotional credit. That does not turn the account into 400 units of freely withdrawable money. It describes two entries with different conditions. The example demonstrates bookkeeping only; it is not an available-offer quotation.

Keep the money you are prepared to risk separate from a displayed credit figure. More available margin does not force a larger position, and the code does not protect against an adverse market move. It is reasonable to decline a promotion if its terms are inconvenient or unclear.
